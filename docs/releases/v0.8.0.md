# shards 0.8.0

MicroVMs now move between machines: between two wired Linux machines a live move stopped
its microVM for 0.21 s at the median, and a CPU baseline is there for machines whose CPUs
differ. Windows on arm64 runs microVMs. Through a context, the folders you bind are your own
machine's, and the other machine's folders, devices and GPUs reach you only as its owner
grants them. Programs that run agents can boot and drive shards microVMs themselves, holding
each agent's input, output, terminal and network reach, and can keep an agent's credentials
outside its microVM. While an agent runs, such a program can pause it, publish its ports,
change what it reaches, plug folders into it and swap in other builds of its agents, harnesses
and MCP servers, with no rebuild and no restart. Agentfiles fetch agents and harnesses from
S3 too, and every fetched source is pinned and reaches only the hosts its directive names.
Waits between shards' own processes, and between machines, now follow what the other side is
doing instead of fixed time-outs. The guest's console is virtio-console. Intel Macs are no
longer a release target: this release has seven binaries.

Before you upgrade: a microVM saved by 0.7.0 doesn't resume in 0.8.0, and a move between
machines needs this release on both (see Changed).

Download `shards` for your machine below and check it against `SHA256SUMS`, as the
[README](https://github.com/hyper-light/shards#install) shows. It is still the only file to
install.

Each item ends with where its evidence is: D-numbers are decisions in
[architecture.md](https://github.com/hyper-light/shards/blob/v0.8.0/docs/design/architecture.md),
M-numbers measurements in
[platform-measurements.md](https://github.com/hyper-light/shards/blob/v0.8.0/docs/research/platform-measurements.md),
and the hex numbers the commits on `main` that made the change.

## New

### Moves between machines

`shards move vm NAME CONTEXT`, and `--live`, now hold up between two machines, not only
between two homes on one machine.

- **Measured between two wired Linux machines** (x86_64, KVM, a gigabit link, n 20 of each):
  a cold move of an alpine microVM left it running nowhere for 0.91 s at the median (p90
  1.12 s), a live one for 0.21 s (p90 0.28 s, at most 0.35 s), in two or three rounds. 40
  of 40 moved. The first move into a fresh home left its microVM running nowhere for 9.9 s,
  as that home fetched the image (PM M497).
- **A move's stream is compressed to suit its link.** It goes in 1 MiB frames, each
  compressed with zstd where that makes it smaller. The level goes up while the move waits
  on the link, and down while it waits on compressing. Between two homes of a Mac Studio, a
  cold move's 82 to 84 MB stream went as 21 to 33 MB. Both machines need the same shards: a
  move to a machine running another build, 0.7.0 included, is refused before anything moves
  (D176 part 6, PM M462; 65e3f462, a2eb10e73).
- **A live move stops copying once its rounds stop shrinking**: at the first round that is
  no smaller than the one before, as well as at a round of 8 MiB or less, after three times
  the first round's bytes, or at 30 rounds. Before, a live move over Wi-Fi of a guest that
  kept writing had not finished after 30 minutes (D176 part 7; 65e3f462).
- **Links to another machine have no fixed time-outs.** In 0.7.0 a connection ended after
  10 s with nothing heard from the other side, and a daemon that hadn't answered in 10 s was
  given up on. Now a command waits as long as you do. When a daemon hasn't answered after
  about a second, or a connection goes quieter than any answer it has given, the command
  says so once on stderr (`shards: NAME: no answer yet from ADDR in T; still waiting (…; ^C
  ends this)`, or `… hasn't been heard for T; still waiting (it may be busy or gone; ^C ends
  this)`), and Ctrl-C ends it. A move whose link goes quiet goes on. A port where nothing
  listens is refused at once, on Linux too. A machine still running 0.7.0 sets a time-out of
  its own, and a link to it still ends after that much silence (D180, D176 part 6;
  084891178, b64a72faf, 40d9af947, d550c32aa).
- **A fresh home fetches the kernel and init as it readies**, before the stream. A fresh
  home's first move had left its microVM running nowhere for 40.7 s, 28.7 s of it a restore
  waiting on that download (PM M462; 20f080a4).
- With `SHARDS_MOVE_TIMES` set, each move reports what it sent, how much of it was
  compressed, the link's rate and its longest silence, and for a live move each round's
  bytes and why the rounds stopped (D176 parts 6 and 7; 65e3f462).

### A CPU baseline for the machines you move between

A microVM resumes only on a machine whose CPU has every feature its guest found. Between an
M3 Ultra (macOS 26.4) and an M4 Max (macOS 26.2), 38 of 40 moves were refused, as each has
features the other lacks (PM M462). `shards configure cpu --baseline CONTEXT[,CONTEXT...]`
asks this machine and each context's machine for the CPU its hypervisor shows a guest, and
tells every microVM this home starts only what all of them have. `shards inspect cpu` shows
it, and `--none` takes it away. Built on macOS and on x86_64 and arm64 Linux, and tested on
a Mac between two homes, where the baseline was all the guest found and went with it when it
moved (PM M497, M499). On x86_64 Windows a move checks the other machine's CPU first, but a
baseline isn't built there yet (D245; 4a8dbf2cc, f617f36e7, 4615b2d77, 0551545cc).

### Live moves on Windows and arm64 Linux

`shards move vm --live NAME CONTEXT` is no longer refused on Windows or on arm64 Linux. On
x86_64 Windows, live moves pass shards' move tests on CI's Windows Server 2025 runners,
between two homes on one machine, including a microVM that keeps writing its memory while it
moves and loses none of it. Each hypervisor now reports what the guest writes during a move:
KVM's dirty-page bitmaps on arm64 (which also catch what the interrupt controller writes into
guest memory), and the hypervisor's own dirty-page tracking on Windows. x86_64 Linux kernels
older than 5.11, which have no dirty rings, use the same bitmap (D176 part 7; d084848df,
c74e18bd5).

### Your own folders through a context

Through a context, `-v` and `--mount type=bind` now bind the folder on the machine where you
typed the command, served from there, relative paths from your working directory, as a bind
of a local folder behaves. `shards plug volume` through a context plugs your folder the same
way. The other machine's own folders, devices and GPUs reach a client only as that machine's
owner grants them: `shards grant client NAME --dir PATH[:ro] --device NAME --gpus`,
`shards revoke client NAME ...` and `shards inspect client NAME`. Over loopback, opening,
reading 4 KiB and closing a file through such a bind took 251 µs at the median against a
local bind's 136, a 64 MiB read ran at 458 MiB/s against 468, and a write at 572 MiB/s
against 2,157 (D228, PM M511; ea34aa231, 5a936953d, 703c6a069).

### Commands

- `shards attach vm NAME`, `shards update vm NAME --cpus N --memory SIZE ...`,
  `shards wait vm NAME` and `shards import vm|image FILE|URL|- [NAME]`: every command a
  microVM needs now has a form of shards' own, and `shards vm ls`, `shards vm rm` and the
  rest are read turned round, as Docker's `image ls` is (D216; 9d03f9a3d).
- `shards read metrics MICROVM` prints a microVM's metrics as an OpenTelemetry request
  (OTLP JSON): CPU, memory, tasks and block I/O of its run, of its workload and of each agent
  and harness, with the memory and task limits set on each, read when you ask, with nothing
  left running to collect them. A run joined to another's microVM (`--network
  container:NAME`) is named by its own ID beside that microVM's, and only its own workload is
  read. A microVM that is paused, or not running, is refused, typed (D229; a920c85b5,
  c13863223, 158b6d0b2, 3c46eb7a9).
- `shards exec vm --for MEMBER NAME COMMAND` runs a command inside one agent or harness, as
  that member runs: its user, namespaces, cgroup, Landlock rules and seccomp filter. What
  the member may not do, the command may not either (D243; 3fe2c9db5, a5a92211f).
- `shards plug volume NAME SOURCE:PATH[:ro] --for MEMBER` plugs a folder into a running
  agent or harness at the path you name. Names that differ only in case are handled on
  macOS and Windows, and edits made on your machine wake programs in the microVM that watch
  with inotify (D235; 03cb7c54e, c654d53a3).
- `-v HOST:PATH:noexec` mounts a folder whose programs can't be run in the microVM, by its
  command or by its agents and harnesses; `exec`, the default, says the opposite. It is
  Podman's word, which Docker's daemon refuses. A run started again keeps it, and `inspect`
  shows it. A program reading a script there as data still can: `python script.py` runs
  (D279; f17b8fb77).
- `SHARDS_DAEMON_JSON` names the one `daemon.json` shards reads for registries, mirrors and
  insecure registries, as `dockerd --config-file` names the one dockerd reads. A file named
  there that is missing is an error that names it (D194; 1c62b32d7).
- The Engine API's stats now carry each vCPU's guest time beside the VM process's CPU time,
  in their `microvm` object, read from each hypervisor (D43; 221c73774, 6396a78b8).
- Every microVM shards makes, from a run, `create`, a tag, a commit, a load or a build,
  appears in Docker's image store as `shards.local/NAME`, as pulls and imports already did
  (D216; 9d03f9a3d).

### Agentfiles

- **Destinations by name.** A network's egress can name the hosts its members reach:
  `NETWORK --egress=HOST:PORT`, a host's name or an IPv4 address, or
  `host.docker.internal` for your own machine at that one port. Members resolve those names
  alone and reach nothing else on that port (D236; 98921fd80, 6576aca74):

  ```dockerfile
  HARNESS codex FROM ./codex
  NETWORK --egress=api.openai.com:443 --egress=chatgpt.com:443 provider FOR codex
  EXPOSE --harnesses=codex 443 AS egress FOR provider
  CONNECT codex WITH codex ON provider
  ```

- **`EXPOSE ... FOR`** may name agents and harnesses as well as networks: the port opens for
  that member alone (D122; 582d90444).
- **`NESTING`** lets an image's own command make boxes (containers, sandboxes) inside its
  microVM, where the run asks for it too (`--security-opt nesting=true`). It is never granted
  beside an agent or harness, and is checked when the image is built and again when it runs
  (D234; b3001d90a, 3ab1eab5d, 9c886608d).
- **Agents and harnesses start as programs expect**: with the image's `ENV`, the run's
  environment, a private writable `HOME` and the working directory the run names. A
  harness's stdin is a stream its owner holds, byte for byte, with its end and abort
  (D240; 239749c60, ac9bb8589, d6ac7e48f, e7825cb32).
- **Agents' and harnesses' output is shown.** When nothing else owns a member's output, the
  daemon keeps it in that member's log and shows it to a client attached to the run on
  stderr, each line prefixed with its member (`[agent main] `). `shards read logs` shows it
  after the run's own output, and `-f` follows it. Everything a member wrote is shown before
  the run's end is reported, a detached run's too (D59, D240, PM M543; 8a2103bdc).
- **Sources from S3.** `AGENT` and `HARNESS` take `s3://BUCKET/KEY` from AWS S3 or another
  S3-compatible store, Cloudflare R2 among them, with `--endpoint`, `--region`,
  `--path-style` and `--version-id`, pinned by `--checksum=sha256:...`, `--version-id` or both.
  The build signs its request with the AWS secrets it is given, sends it unsigned with none,
  and uses the host's own AWS credentials only where the directive says
  `--credentials=aws-default`. The object is recorded with its version and SHA-256 in the
  build's provenance (D54, D88; a9f17f701, b8daa959b).
- **Every fetched source is pinned, and reaches only what it names.** An http(s) source of
  `AGENT` or `HARNESS` needs `--checksum=sha256:...` (see Changed), and BuildKit's HTTP
  authorization secrets (`HTTP_AUTH_HEADER_<host>`, `HTTP_AUTH_TOKEN_<host>`) authorize its
  fetch, kept to that host's domain through redirects. A fetch for `AGENT`, `HARNESS`,
  `SKILL` or `MCP` connects to its source's origin and to each one `--redirect=HOST[:PORT]`
  names, and nowhere else: a redirect elsewhere, Git's included, and a submodule at another
  origin are refused before anything is sent there, the refusal saying what to write. A
  source named by an `ARG` is checked once its stage has expanded it. Dockerfile `ADD` is
  unchanged (D54, D56, PM M608; a9f17f701, 56fd3bc69, 2180a1b83, 0eada3856, 198fc204f).

### For programs that run shards

`shards-api` (`crates/api`) lets a program boot microVMs through the `shards` binary and talk
to them, every refusal typed (D215, PM M463, M464, M465, M515; 86b4422cb). With it, a
program can now:

- boot an Agentfile's image under shards-init with no daemon, one `shards` process hosting
  each run (D231; 6aa822fef). A run now restores its image's template and takes a warm
  microVM from its pool, as the daemon's runs do (D253; aad75a06b, 7d01b8aef), and can be
  stopped while it is still booting, which ends its microVM and every process of the run
  (D231; ed1136305, b0483fee5, 0968c836e);
- keep its images' warm microVMs in pools sized as the daemon sizes its own, by their runs'
  demand, each charged to the program's own room before it starts (`ImagePools::new(room,
  keep)`): a claim the room refuses starts nothing, `release` ends the coldest until the
  memory it asks for is free, `reserve` holds one for one run, and `held` gives each pool's
  claimed room and what each warm microVM uses. A pool is refilled once the run that took its
  warm microVM has started, not as it is taken. A start from such a pool, its room holding
  two warm microVMs, read its program's first output 5.210 ms after its call at the median
  of 100, p90 5.710, p99 6.079 and at most 6.084, on a Mac Studio (M4 Max, macOS 26.2) at
  26c1ea5df, and 5.328, 5.835, 6.181 and 6.371 ms in a second run of that build, 100 starts
  with no other build between them (PM M593, below). Its comparison with a start that
  restores the image's template, by turns on a quiet host and a loaded one, and its runs on
  KVM and WHP are owed (D253, PM M612; 9cfded7b7, d50ddf2ae, ae94b3ab8, 56be44d37);
- know that the process hosting a run holds its three standard streams and nothing else
  the program had open (D248; 9bdd4a7ec);
- hold each agent's and harness's stdin, stdout and stderr, give it a terminal of its own and
  size it, and exec into it (D240, D243, D244; e7825cb32, d689fa652, c2ecd9e81), and give
  it arguments that follow its image's command, as a harness's session flags do (D256;
  44e3c48a1, 779964edf);
- keep or drop each member's network destinations before the microVM starts (D246;
  7a4ef5c02). A run that keeps nothing past the microVM still gets the network its image
  links its members to, so a harness and its provider run (D250; 9b4bdb802, 3a16dbd92);
- tell a run the room its budget leaves it, and read its microVM's cost in its stats (D241;
  3ce473b31), hold a run to a CPU rate in milli-CPU (D247; 9bdd4a7ec, e1eed9e1c), and hold
  a microVM it booted itself to one, over that microVM's vsock socket (D255; d84275b12,
  7667087ba);
- learn how each run ended and why: a command that never started ends failed, with the
  microVM's own words, and a microVM that failed to serve its run says so (D249, D258;
  9b4bdb802, 67cbbfec3, fc225a807, a0e12422e, 8354b408a);
- save a run's microVM whole, to a place only the run's owner names (D259; 09c4bf5b8,
  8a9fa413d);
- give a microVM GPUs by their exact identity: a PCI address, a Metal registry ID, or this
  boot's DXGI LUID on Windows (D232; 08bb8fda1, 1fd078bfb);
- start the VM process itself, inside its own cgroup or Job object (D233; 84274050f,
  301e5bddb);
- carry `shards` inside its own binary, `shards` writing its VM and network processes out
  beside the copy, signed as the program signs its own (D237; f2a42e98a, d42407036);
- reach a microVM's members over one encrypted connection whose TLS ends at the host: their
  stdio as lanes, execs, each member's state, its end, its signals and its terminal's size,
  and every member's log, kept within the run's one log cap (D238, PM M540; 2a3b62356,
  c9630c6b4, 4c50a8744, cf9e4590b, f0a651428, cc36a60d3, 789f9ab08, edca0b3f6). Each run's
  endpoint has a key of its own, made after its microVM's restore and kept on the host: the
  program pins its fingerprint, told as the run starts, the endpoint takes the program's key
  alone, and either end refuses any other key at the handshake, typed (`NotEnrolled`). A
  moved microVM's endpoint makes a new key where it lands (D238, D176; 898a02609). A run it
  boots binds that connection's endpoint too (D252; be6656f17, 2c5105cc3), and a lane can
  reach a socket of the run's own command, carried over vsock, a cut stream reset at the
  other end and a half-closed one ended in order (D251, PM M559; 07831ec23, 5387a353f,
  8754b451f, 275b4e178, c1aba3c6c). Where the program asks for them, the endpoint of a run it
  boots serves its members' stdin, stdout and stderr as lanes too, as a daemon's run's
  endpoint does, each named by its member's generation, so that a member started again is
  followed onto its next (D252; 10c82ef2c). Those lanes are charged to the run's budget as the
  daemon charges its runs' streams, and the connection they share once, however many lanes it
  carries, with its sockets' queues at the sizes the OS reports for them. A run with no room
  for its connection or for its members' first lanes is refused `Capacity` before any member
  starts, its microVM never handed the run, and a restart whose next generation has none is
  refused before it starts (D252; d8935aca1, 32b27e6c3, 3c76f6c05);
- serve its own MCP server to a harness in the microVM over that same connection, one lane
  per MCP connection, with no network grant for it (D254; c59eecec7, 6f786c91c,
  af8f1ebe5). The endpoint's first report names the members whose MCP bridge it serves, so
  that `mcp:N` is among a controller's lanes from the start; the connection's protocol is now
  `shards-control/2`, and a controller of the first version is refused at its handshake
  (D254; f99a3900b);
- ask whether this host runs microVMs, and why not, without booting one: no backend for this
  machine, the hypervisor turned off, KVM missing or denied, a VM given no nested
  virtualization, or a capability the backend lacks, each typed (D262; a08900ccc);
- build an Agentfile into an image with no daemon, told its progress as it goes and a
  refusal in the build's own words, typed, with a policy's messages and the lines an error is
  at; the image is pinned by its ID (D263; 3efe78910, 7cf745fbc);
- pause a run and resume it: the VMM holds every vCPU, and the run's memory, devices and
  connections are kept. On x86_64 KVM its guest is told, so that its watchdogs take the pause
  for one (D267; 0218d886d, 3613d696b);
- change a run while it runs, with no rebuild and no restart: publish a port and let one go
  (D269; 85c46c666); grant and revoke what it reaches past its microVM, within its image's
  grants, for the whole microVM (D273; 4c6232102, 27bb01b9f) or for one member, whose revoked
  connections are reset (D278; f6e0b057b, 3f6734697); and plug a host folder or a volume of
  its home into the members it names, beneath the roots the run's spec grants (D271;
  644b6d261);
- give a folder to the members it names as they start, at any path, so that a harness
  starts in its project, and starts there again as it is started again or swapped: each
  start takes the working directory it names, else the one its run gave, else its image's.
  A member that can't enter its working directory is refused, typed, as its guest finds it,
  the refusal naming the member, the directory and the error: as it first starts, as it
  starts again, and as a swap starts it again. A working directory its image holds works as
  before (D277, PM M610; 325457a88, 806841601, a25e25512, 88a9b4726, 07aa5aa5e);
- swap an agent, harness or MCP server into a running microVM by its digest. Each is built
  once on its own, as an OCI artifact; its image is plugged in read-only and mapped DAX, so
  every microVM of a host that runs that version maps the same pages; and its member starts
  again on it at its next start, or at once where asked. A run holding such components is
  saved with them (D270; d419ce747, d1d3bcd97, e12df9e4d, c0aee1b38, 600ab556d, 021b6435c,
  f00d9756d). A member swapped at once starts again with its last start's arguments, or with
  those the swap gives, so that a harness keeps its session's flags (D270; c91fe3614); and a
  swap whose member can't enter its working directory, or whose run has no room for its next
  start's streams, is refused by that cause (D277, D252; 88a9b4726, 88a29210a);
- keep an agent's volumes on its machine across its sessions, list them with the bytes each
  holds, and clear them (D281; 4a5178d9a). A volume any run holds, a daemon's or a program's,
  is refused its clearing until that run has ended (D281; 9cf44e88e, f00c87453). A run holds
  its own anonymous volumes, its image's `VOLUME`s, as it holds its named ones, so that no
  clearing, `shards remove volume` or `shards prune volume` takes one while it runs, and each
  goes as its run ends, its lock file too (D281; 3c76f6c05, f461e7874). A volume can
  be given a bound, an agent's the disk its admission gave it: its share's server holds the
  bound as files are written, a write past it cut short and the next refused `ENOSPC`, and
  `df` in the microVM shows it. The listing gives each volume's bound and what its files take
  by that measure (D281; eabb179cd, 05c87fbba, d704727d5, c9993bfb6, a3025e473);
- connect a member to a network shared with the program's other runs as it runs, and
  disconnect it, one grant at a time: the peer it reaches or is reached by, one protocol's
  ports, and which way. Each grant is held within the member's image's grants and its
  ceiling, each flow at both of its ends, and a member is answered its own name and the names
  its grants name, no others. A port of the run's address on such a network is one member's:
  a second member granted in on it is refused `Unheld`, and the member granted is reached
  there whichever member holds that port at the microVM's own address (D122). A run's names are
  withdrawn first as its microVM ends, and from each other run as its link there closes, so
  that no run is answered the name of one that has gone. The program can make such a network
  by its name, as `shards network create NAME` makes one, and remove it, with no daemon; a
  removal is refused, typed, while any run is on the network. One with IPv6 is refused for
  now (D285; cbc4df540, cf9abc195, e9fc17bd8, 94b5276d8, 57f1e6a39, 4e3fdf62b, db4ca2154,
  4348c8c1b, fad34cf85, d768c671c);
- read what a microVM it booted itself has used, each vCPU's guest time beside its VM
  process's own CPU and memory, over that microVM's vsock socket, and its final use as it ends
  (D260; 867053aa1, 1e2d403ec).

### Credentials that stay outside the microVM

An image's member can be granted a credential by name, and the microVM holds only a
placeholder. As a request leaves for a host the grant names, shards' network process ends
its TLS with a certificate from the run's own authority, which only the granted members
trust. It fills in the real value, and masks the value wherever the reply echoes it, in a
gzip-, deflate- or zstd-coded body too. This works over HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/2. The value is
leased from its owner as a request needs it and released as the run ends; a request whose
lease is refused is answered 503. Claude Code's
and Codex's sign-ins are kept the same way: their tokens are taken as they are issued, and
their refreshes and revocations answered, outside the microVM (D242; 485fe0c21, 314c3cfc7,
e1f1d1f0a, fa88b2328, 51e674604, 41d7b6fb4, d0a6dbf0c, 8d4b53874, 3114e082f, 1f190f888).
Codex's sign-in is tested through the injector as Claude Code's is: its device flow's tokens
taken outside, its ID token reaching the microVM inert, a refresh asked on two connections at
once answered once, and its re-login's and logout's revocations answered outside, the kept
sign-in still filled in after the microVM's logout (D242; 47c7d1e64). Of the other eight
harnesses kiln runs, read from their own builds, goose's ChatGPT sign-in is Codex's own
client and is granted as Codex's; the seven others still sign in inside the microVM, each
for a reason the survey records (D242; f7b2ba3f8). A saved microVM's memory can be scanned
for any value it was given, the scan reading only the data of a save's sparse files, on
Windows as on Unix (D259; 8a9fa413d, 016e98755, 2e5183364, 1dfaafff0). This is new, and
parts of it haven't run with real microVMs or real accounts yet (see Known limits).

vsock connections are now credited before they run out, and lent a window of up to 4 MiB
where their guest uses it (D12; 1e448c7ec, b1a011876). A microVM's engine connection can now
go through a ring in its guest's memory instead of vsock. Its tests pass on real microVMs
on macOS and Linux, and in part on x86_64 Windows (below), and the device refuses an offer
longer than its slot (D230; 261412b01, 032b63b1c, b81d707a9, e072a886f). What either change gains
is not in the measurements record yet.

## Platforms

### arm64 Windows

On arm64 Windows 11 (24H2, build 26100.3915 or later) with the Windows Hypervisor Platform
turned on, `shards` runs microVMs. It uses the same daemon, images, volumes, networks and
snapshots as the x86_64 build.

- Snapshots keep every vCPU's registers and the interrupt controller's state. A restore
  holds the guest's clock until its vCPUs run again, so the guest's time never jumps.
- A restore maps the pages the microVM's start touched before the guest needs them, as on
  x86_64 Windows.
- On a machine without the Windows Hypervisor Platform, `shards` says "no hypervisor runs on
  this host". Before, it gave an error about a missing device.

So far this has been tested on Windows 11 Pro arm64 running emulated on a Mac, with Windows'
own hypervisor (PM M414). There, shards booted its kernel as a standard user, and restored
three separate processes from one snapshot. Busy microVMs of 2 and 8 vCPUs went on past their
snapshots and restores with their clocks and interrupts intact. The whole test suite has not
passed there yet (see Known limits). CI builds arm64 Windows on arm64 Windows runners and runs
its tests there; those runners have no hypervisor, so their microVM tests are skipped
(D209, PM M423; cdd2c031f).

### Windows

- **PCI devices on Windows Server.** Where Windows' hypervisor allows device assignment
  (Discrete Device Assignment: Windows Server on hardware with an IOMMU), a microVM's kernel
  can be given a PCI device you have dismounted from the host with
  `Dismount-VMHostAssignableDevice`, named by the instance ID `Get-VMHostAssignableDevice`
  lists: `shards run vm --kernel vmlinux --vfio 'PCIP\VEN_...' ...`. The host's IOMMU
  limits its DMA to the microVM's own memory, on x86_64 and arm64 alike. Every Windows host
  tested so far refuses device assignment, and shards passes on their answer. A device can't
  be handed on to a program in the microVM on Windows (`--device`, `plug function`), nor
  plugged into a microVM already running: Microsoft documents assignment only for a VM
  given its device before it starts (D154, D214; 9e0279a45, 3d8cac1ce).
- **Plugs, moves and restarts pass with real microVMs on CI's Windows Server 2022 and 2025
  runners**: `shards plug` and `unplug` for disks and folders, `shards grow disk`, saved and
  live moves between homes with a volume's owners, modes, symlinks and device nodes kept, and
  restarts that reuse a stopped microVM's kept writable layer (D214, D190; b697d6b55).
  0.7.0's notes said `plug volume` was refused on Windows and `plug disk` untested there.
- **Hard links from Windows clients**: when you build or `cp` from a Windows machine, files
  with several names arrive as hard links, as they do from macOS and Linux, and the archive
  is byte for byte what a Unix client sends for the same tree. Docker's Windows client still
  sends each name's whole file (D180; 3c8b8eac2).
- Of the 129 build tests, 125 now build for Windows and run there, and the build context
  tests and the kept `.git` directory test run against what buildx's own file walker records
  on Windows (3b4978508, 7176e6f62, ca6100217).
- A daemon held to some of the host's CPUs gives its microVMs those alone, as on Linux
  (D125, PM M495; e2820ce9).
- How many threads a process may run is derived from what Windows charges each thread, not
  a fixed figure (D181, PM M529; cacbd8883).
- **A microVM held to a CPU rate on Windows Server is held to it.** There Windows' hypervisor
  charges a vCPU's guest time to none of its process's threads, so a quarter-CPU hold let a
  busy vCPU run 88% of the time. Whether a VM process's CPU time holds its vCPUs' is now
  learned from that process's own clocks, never from the Windows edition (D265, PM M602;
  697e12402, 14e5231a6).
- **A vsock connection's Windows side is a named pipe each way.** Windows' Unix sockets lose
  what a peer hadn't read when the other end closes, and a named pipe keeps it (PM M603,
  M609): pipes are there so that every byte a guest sent before its end reaches the program
  on the host, and that program's write after the guest's end fails at once. A program that
  connects to a microVM's vsock path (`--vsock PATH`) now reads the file there for its two
  pipes' names and opens the way down, then the way up; `CONNECT` and the rest of the
  protocol over them are unchanged, and shards-api does this itself. A program that listens
  for its guest at `PATH_PORT` names a pair of its own there the same way (see Changed).
  Their first run on WHP found two faults, both fixed. A pipe's cancelled call was waited for
  on its handle, which Windows sets only as the call's packet is taken from the device's
  completion port, by the one thread that was waiting, so the device hung; each call now
  names an event of its own, and a connection holds four handles. And a wait that a packet
  ended with nothing ready returned early; it now goes on for the time left, as poll(2)'s
  does. Since then, on x86_64 WHP, the VMM's 62 pipe and vsock tests pass, and so do all six
  of shards' vsock tests, a guest that ends its stream giving its host every byte first among
  them (D272; 3101b23ce, 08338e713, b7e1eea06, c26e40d7f, 0bef06525, 10b7a89f4, 341b7ce6a,
  41d6ea3c2).

### Linux

- `shards plug function` passes nested on arm64 Linux (an M5 Max) for a program that doesn't
  run as root, the device named by its PCI name or by its VFIO device node
  (`/dev/vfio/devices/vfioN`) through iommufd. The VMM's part of plugging it took 602 µs at
  the median and 626 at p90 (n 30). The first plug into a microVM took 170 ms: VFIO pins all
  of the microVM's memory for the device, faulting in what the microVM hasn't touched yet,
  and a microVM booted with a device pays the same as it starts. The whole command hasn't
  been timed yet (D214, PM M430; a45a7fca8, 72a2d1864, 0cef5fda3).

### macOS

- The CPU baseline above is built and tested on Hypervisor.framework. A guest there is shown
  less of its CPU than macOS names for the host, so a move between Macs is judged by what the
  hypervisor shows a guest, not by what macOS names (D245, PM M497, M499; 9a6f5ac59,
  abcea5f5).
- The intermittent "no grant request" stall and the CLI killed at launch were each measured

## Faster

- **Pooled runs.** A warm microVM makes room in its descriptor table for its run before it
  is ready. Handing a run to a warm microVM took 13.53 ms at the median and now 0.14 ms, and
  a run is usable 5.82 ms after the request at the median, down from 19.17 ms (Fedora on an
  Intel Core i9-14900HX, KVM, at load 20 to 26). Against the build before, a pooled
  `run --rm alpine true` took 15.1 ms less at the median of 150 pairs, at load 64 (D26,
  PM M470; e852e43da).
- **seccomp and device filters.** The guest kernel now compiles BPF programs. A filter's cost
  to a system call fell from 30.6 to 10.5 ns on KVM, 36.5 to 20.9 ns on Hypervisor.framework
  and 262.5 to 62.5 ns on Windows; a device filter's from 11.0 ns to nothing measurable on
  KVM, 18.2 to 2.6 ns on macOS and 200 to 25 ns on Windows. Loading a filter takes 11 to
  20 µs more, and on macOS a restored run that starts a workload holds 432 KiB more and
  starts 206 µs later (D227, PM M489; 36e475360).
- **Build policies** (`--policy`, `buildx policy eval`, `policy test`) with many rules
  compile faster: a 6,000-rule policy parsed and compiled in 299.8 ms at the median (p90
  308.6, max 313.8), down from 779.5 ms (p90 807.0, max 825.2), and a test of a deeply nested
  `every` policy took 1,736 ms at the median, down from 3,372 ms (M5 Max, n 30 and n 20,
  builds alternating; PM M414; 84ea5dbf0). `glob.match` patterns that took 1.7 s, 6.5 s and
  more than two and five minutes now take 5 to 77 ms, and numbers with millions of digits or
  huge exponents no longer run a policy out of memory (D98, PM M510, M524, M526, M537;
  825a7c6c3, d5707dbca, 944f16220, a03b37113).
- **Pushes** send as many blobs at once as pay on each path, learned as they go, where 0.7.0
  sent five, and in as few requests as the registry takes, so a registry that refuses a large
  request body can be pushed to. On one host 16 uploads at once carried 7.3 times what one
  did (D226, PM M488; 0d1fb8c2b, ef4030536). What it saves against real registries isn't
  measured yet.
- **Folders shared from Windows** no longer copy a directory's whole listing into memory each
  time a program in the microVM opens it: 100 open directories of 2,000 entries each had held
  10.77 MB (D38; d2cc3f8be).
- **`shards inspect`** with many names reads the image store once per command instead of once
  per name. How much time this saves hasn't been measured (6c9b772df).
- **A smaller VM process.** Each microVM runs in a VM process of its own, and the pages of
  that process's file its start reads stay resident in every microVM. On Linux its start
  reads each of its relative relocations first, and those took 24 bytes each; they are now
  packed as LLD's RELR packs them, up to 63 in a word. The VM process that serves GPUs is
  linked as before. A VM process built without the GPU's renderer, which already refused
  every GPU, no longer carries the GPU device's code either. Nothing a microVM does changes,
  and how much either change saves hasn't been measured (D36; 13fc49770).
- **A program's runs start with less in their way.** A warm microVM now reads its guest
  kernel's version, compiles the security filters its run will install for that kernel, and
  makes its run's agent-control key while it waits for its run: on a loaded M4 Max, a
  start's planning, the first two its costly part, had taken 10.8 to 24 ms. Every run host
  checks its VM's binary, and makes its microVM's writable layer, on threads of their own
  beside the rest of its start, and removes that layer's file name off the start's way
  (D252, D253; 165d5c2f0, 3488abfa9, c4b0af14f, eb7a86d76, 1b8d1cddd). The layer's own
  thread was measured against the build before it, through shards-api on a Mac Studio (M4
  Max, 14 CPUs, 36 GiB, macOS 26.2): dcac51807 against 26c1ea5df by turns, 100 starts of
  each kind in each build, and 10 more of each timed step by step (PM M593). Before it, a
  launch waited for its layer 1.570 ms at the median where its start boots its guest, 1.441
  where it also saves its template and 0.290 where it restores one; with it, none of the 40
  timed launches waited more than 0.011 ms. A start that boots its guest answered 1.864 ms
  sooner at the median, a fall its 95% intervals show: 73.011, 76.992, 80.454 and 85.908 ms
  at p50, p90, p99 and its slowest, against 74.875, 77.804, 81.498 and 84.237 before (100
  starts give a p99 or a slowest no interval). One that saves its template answered 1.308 ms
  sooner at the median, a fall its interval doesn't show, as its save spreads it: 196.578,
  208.006, 216.419 and 223.142 ms, against 197.886, 208.676, 215.528 and 219.769. A start
  from a warm microVM has no layer on its way in either build: its program's first output
  came 5.158 ms after its call at the median before and 5.210 after (p90 5.714 and 5.710,
  p99 5.946 and 6.079, slowest 6.436 and 6.084), over shards' 5 ms target in both. One start
  of each kind timed step by step at 26c1ea5df (n 1, so its spread unmeasured) shows where:
  a warm microVM took its run 0.158 ms after the call and said it had started at 4.958 ms,
  so the target is missed inside the microVM's own start of the run, past the run host's
  steps. Of the run host's steps before a launch, making the run's security took longest,
  5.484 to 6.077 ms a kind, and a warm microVM's host takes it before its run asks (PM
  M593). What the other steps save hasn't been measured: both builds had them.

## Changed

- **A microVM saved by 0.7.0 doesn't resume in 0.8.0.** A save (`save vm`, a stopped
  microVM's move, a directory given to `shards restore`) holds the shards-init that was
  running, which speaks only its own build's contract with its host. shards now records who
  saved each, and before anything runs refuses one whose shards-init is another build's,
  naming the version that saved it. Start it again from its image: its volumes are kept, its
  memory's state is not. A move to a machine running another build is refused the same way
  (D176; c4e5a1ab9, a2eb10e73).
- **Seven binaries.** Intel Macs are unsupported, and this release has no
  `shards-darwin-amd64` (b4918cd8e).
- **The guest kernel** is `kernel-6.18.48-836568f8ad2c`: it compiles BPF programs (above,
  D227; 36e475360), its virtio-mem driver lets go of an online memory block a part at a time,
  giving way to a changed request (7b807bcfb), it runs as many vCPUs as the VMM gives it,
  255 on x86_64 and 123 on arm64 (D13; 9933b02bf), a file in a DAX-shared folder whose
  size the host changes is mapped again safely, so a reader sees the host's end of it
  (D189, D235, PM M550; 379d59a6e), its console shows the whole boot log, and it tells its
  host when it panics, through pvpanic (D261; 875f1d3d1, 4a304be9e, 0558db043, 1f71dea19).
- **The guest's console is virtio-console (`hvc0`)** on every hypervisor and architecture;
  the emulated 16550 UART is gone. `run vm --kernel` now defaults to `--cmdline
  "console=hvc0 panic=-1"`, where it was `console=ttyS0 earlycon panic=-1`, so a command line
  of your own should name `console=hvc0` in place of `console=ttyS0`. A kernel booted with
  `--init` whose command line names a console the machine hasn't got (`console=ttyAMA0`, say)
  still runs its init, with no console: the initramfs now carries `/dev/null`, which the
  init's runtime opens for the standard streams its kernel gave it none of. Before, the init
  ended at once, the kernel panicked and the microVM reset, its console silent (D261;
  6a4b27d13). A guest that panics before its console is up tells its host through pvpanic,
  and shards reads what it printed from its printk ring. In a first, preliminary run on a Mac
  (9 rounds each way), a MiB written to the console took 217 to 251 ms of the guest's time
  against the UART's 2,080 to 2,193, and 282 to 318 ms of host CPU against 2,041 to 2,183
  (D261, PM M588; 231ba114f, 83d6e8613).
- **An x86_64 guest restarts through its ACPI reset register.** The emulated i8042 keyboard
  controller, there only so that a guest's restart reached the VMM, is gone, and shards adds
  `reboot=a` after your own command line's options (D268; 8b06b6ea4).
- **Waits come from what the host shows, not from Docker's figures.** A kill waits as long as
  the microVM goes on ending, which grows with what it holds: 0.3 s for 16 GiB at the median,
  5.6 s beside a host's every CPU busy (D217, PM M480, M481, M482; c9bc0558c). A restart
  policy's waits come from what restarts cost on the host (D219, PM M484; 421fb3b65). A
  registry's waits are learned from the path to it, as TCP learns its retransmission
  timeout: of 39,260 requests to eleven registries, 25 were never answered, each a stuck
  request rather than a registry down (D222, PM M486; e7c253f16). A restart that can reuse
  a stopped microVM's kept layer goes over it or packs it, whichever its own host's restarts
  have shown quicker, learned as it runs: measured, the two crossed at 6.8 to 7.9 MiB on a
  Mac, 7.7 and 8.0 MiB on a Windows machine and 5.1 MiB on Linux (D190, PM M496;
  3afa74c62). The VMM's and the runtime's other fixed figures were audited: each is now a
  spec's, the hypervisor's, derived from what it bounds, or marked as owed a measurement
  (9bbccb203, 876922312, c38fdd13e, 453a3d4d0, 7ddceb974).
- **Waits follow what the other side is doing.** A kill now waits while the microVM's
  process does anything toward its end: runs at all, frees memory, or is held back by its
  host. Before, it waited only while that process used more CPU than an idle microVM, which
  cut short a teardown that ran slower than that. Network and share processes are followed
  to their ends the same way (D217, PM M546; 21cb5acb0). A registry that stops answering is ridden
  out no longer than the longest trouble its path has come through (D222, PM M555, M558;
  69be6d1c1, ea6f4b53b). `shards stop daemon` returns once its daemon has let go of its
  home, and a second stop waits for the first (D185, PM M542, M545, M549, M553;
  3ac9b172c). Where a process shards waits on is waiting in its kernel (Linux's `D`, macOS's
  uninterruptible wait, and on Windows a wait on its host's paging: a page read in or freed,
  a pool allocation, or its kernel stack paged out), it is taken as held by its host only
  while the host's disks complete I/O: a wait on another process's work at the disk, such as
  a btrfs commit that two stops share, goes on, and a wait that no device serves, a dead NFS
  mount's, is given up on as a stall. On Windows such a wait had been taken as held whatever
  its host's disks did (D215; 927ba0119, 2726a8deb). The daemon's own asks of a microVM, to
  give back what it holds cold of its files, to hear its budget's room, to read its metrics
  and the rest of its quiet work, are waited for while its process runs: past the slowest
  answer each kind has had, the daemon looks at that process once each such span, and ends
  the ask, saying so, only when it has run nothing at all since the look before. In 0.7.0 a
  give-back or a room told that had no answer within 10 s was ended, so a microVM that its
  host slowed past its budget answered too late (PM M467). Each wait begins before its ask,
  so that an answer that comes at once teaches it too (D188; 6fc62c231, 07aa5aa5e).
- **Every QUIC link shards makes, and the in-VM server's TLS, use AES-256-GCM alone**, both
  ways. A peer offering no AES-256-GCM is refused (D239; e12d5b96e).
- **A setting shards-init doesn't know is refused.** A guest's init that doesn't know a
  setting its host sends now refuses the run before applying anything. Before, it ignored
  that setting, so a guest of another build (`shards configure guest`) could drop one
  silently (D257; a0b46929b, f2c95eb51).
- **Binds through a context are the client's folders** (above). A command that would have the
  other machine's CLI read or write that machine's files, such as `--env-file`, `cp`, a
  kernel booted directly or a build of a folder there, is refused through a context (D228;
  ea34aa231).
- **Agentfiles that can't be held are refused.** On a TCP port that one member reaches by
  name (a destination, or a remote MCP server), every other member granted that port must be
  granted the same names. An image where it isn't fails its build, naming both members, and
  remote MCP servers beside another member's other grants on one port, which built before,
  are refused alike (D236; 98921fd80).
- **`shards run container X` and `create container X`**, where `X` is a shards microVM, are
  refused and say so: `shards run vm X` runs it (D216; 9d03f9a3d).
- **A slow start is waited for.** In 0.7.0 a microVM still booting after a minute was given
  up on, and on an emulated arm64 Windows host every one given up on was still booting
  (PM M424). Now the daemon waits on a starting microVM while its process is alive, then
  past the slowest start it has seen, and gives up only after a stretch that long in which
  the microVM ran no CPU and its host wasn't holding it back. `daemon.log` says once how far
  into its start a slow one is (D209; fcc674d6a, 76c4407b0).
- **`shards configure disk`** is refused through a context: the share of the machine's disk
  that shards keeps is the owner's to set, as `configure guest` already was (D186;
  b8270370a).
- **`shards invite client` needs `--valid DURATION`** (`10m`, `2h`). An invitation lasts as
  long as its owner says, with no default, lets one machine join once, and can be revoked
  until it is used. One made by an earlier shards, under its fixed hour, has lapsed (D180;
  30645fa69).
- **An http(s) source of `AGENT` or `HARNESS` needs `--checksum=sha256:...`**, as what a URL
  serves can change without notice; bytes that hash to another are refused. A redirect to
  another origin is refused until `--redirect` names it (above; D54; a9f17f701).
- **A guest's last bytes wait for their reader, and a host's connection for its guest.**
  vsock dropped what a host hadn't read 2 s after the guest ended a connection, and reset a
  host's connection that its guest hadn't answered within 2 s, figures taken from
  Firecracker. A busy, paused or rate-held reader lost output, and a paused or rate-held guest
  was refused a connection it would have answered. Now an ended connection keeps its bytes
  until they are read, within the window it had, and a host's connection waits for its guest
  on no clock; where every slot is taken, the one left unanswered longest gives its place up
  (D264, D266; a81091643, 277300976).
- **Build fetches, policy signatures and log shippers learn their waits from their paths**,
  as registry pulls do, and a build's git fetch is asked again through what the network
  drops. A harness built from git had failed `github.com: resolving: timed out` on a network
  whose resolver failed on and off, and a pack that GitHub sends after its 200 was cut as a
  silence and asked for again for as long as the build ran (D276, PM M607; 6ecd320fd,
  4e73fbea4, d756d2a3b).
- **`--opt size` bounds a volume.** `shards create volume --opt size=SIZE NAME` was refused
  in 0.7.0 (`quota size requested but no quota support`, the words of Docker's local driver
  where its file system has no quotas). Now the server that shares the volume with a microVM
  holds the bound as files are written, needing no quotas of the host's: a write past it is
  cut short and the next refused `ENOSPC`, and `df` in the microVM shows the bound and what is
  used. Each name takes one allocation unit of the host's file system, each file its length in
  whole units, and their extended attributes theirs, so a sparse file counts its whole
  length. Every member and microVM the volume is plugged into shares one count. A bound is
  held for a named volume alone, and changes only while no run holds the volume (D281;
  eabb179cd, 05c87fbba, d704727d5).
- **A name a microVM or a member isn't granted is answered NXDOMAIN**, where it was answered
  REFUSED: by the microVM's resolver, and by the agents' resolver within it, over UDP and TCP.
  musl's stub resolver drops REFUSED, so such a lookup in an Alpine-based microVM waited out
  musl's 5 s timeout and failed `EAI_AGAIN`; it now ends at once, `EAI_NONAME`. glibc's ended
  at once either way. The answer carries no SOA record, so that no resolver caches it past a
  later grant of that name. REFUSED stays for what is no query of one question, and for a
  question past its asker's share of those in flight (D46, D59, D79, D278; 4348c8c1b,
  db4ca2154).
- **A `--rm` run's removal is not synced.** Its record says `--rm`, so one that a power loss
  brings back is removed as the daemon next starts, before it sees any name, as dockerd's
  restore removes its AutoRemove containers. A stop no longer waits on a sync for its `--rm`
  runs, nor does any `--rm` run's end: on btrfs that sync was a whole transaction's commit on
  every stop's path. Every other removal is synced as before (D27, D215; 927ba0119).
- **On Windows, a microVM's vsock path is a file naming two pipes**, where it was a Unix
  socket: a program that connected to it as a socket opens the pipes it names instead, and one
  that listened at `PATH_PORT` names a pair there (above; D272).

## Fixed

- **Found by review before this release.** Twelve adversarial reviews of main, on 2026-11-13
  and 14, reported 134 findings: among them tokens that could pass the credential injector's
  mask, a command run in a member that could take a wider filesystem grant than the member
  had, a disk grown twice at once truncating its filesystem, and a move's stream able to
  make a link leading out of its staging directory. Each finding's report gives its status,
  the test that failed before its fix and passes after, and any run still owed. At this
  revision all are fixed but four: three in part (devices-08, migrate-10, waits-12), and one
  refuted (control-06) ([the findings](https://github.com/hyper-light/shards/blob/v0.8.0/docs/bugs/2026-11-13/README.md)).
- **Contexts reached past their grants.** The review found that a client's command behind a
  context's choice (`--context default client grant alice --dir /`) granted the client this
  machine's root, as the machine judged the command by its first word; that on Windows a
  build file a client named `C:context` was written outside its copy, over the daemon's own
  files; and that a removed client kept the authority of its open connections. A client's
  command is now judged as this machine's shards runs it, a context's choice from a client is
  refused, a name Windows reads as a path is refused, and a client's key is judged again at
  each operation, its removal closing its connections at once (D180; cbcbc0ad1, bbab70a66,
  059831a0b). A client's build now names no path on this machine but its own copies'
  (5ac001f91). As first landed, after 0.7.0, that check refused every build through a
  context on Unix, and then every one on a machine whose home's path holds a link, as
  macOS's `/var` does; both are fixed (e1e4cbe1e, 3415c4993).
- **Builds held to a memory limit** (Linux). In 0.7.0 the process that writes a limited
  build's files outside its limit was refused as an unknown command, so builds wrote their
  files inside the limit and their writing steps were slower. Now the writer runs, a step's
  changes reach it a chunk at a time, writes pass through memory the build and the writer
  share, and a held build's caches are read and written outside its limit too. A build held
  to a limit short of its peak took no longer than the same build unlimited, beyond its
  rounds' spread, in all 27 host and build pairs measured (eight of CI's runners and a Fedora
  host) and on a Mac. Changes made since that run haven't been measured yet (D188, PM M442;
  9955856ee, 0c1d5901a, 0849ecb3a, 0cba57cf7, d9c42120).
- **Commands lost under load.** Of many concurrent `shards exec` commands, some could hang:
  a resumed guest could refuse an exec's connection for a busy lock, and a signal could wait
  behind a guest that reads slowly. Both are fixed, and a lost connection is now said and its
  process ended (D16, PM M541; 0d4f20288, 2944d9ab5, 3607aad11, 782275c26).
- **Contexts.** A command run with `--context default`, or under `SHARDS_CONTEXT` or
  `DOCKER_CONTEXT` naming another machine, sent shards' own helper processes to that machine,
  so the daemon never started here. Shards' own helpers now always run where they start
  (D180; f125ef3a8). Once `shards remove context` returns, nothing more reaches that machine
  through it (d91a62737, eecee5b12).
- **A build context changed while it is sent.** If a file or folder in a build's context was
  replaced by a symlink after shards listed the context and before it sent it, the build could
  send files from outside the context. Each folder is now opened inside the one above it and
  never through a link, on every platform, and on Windows unpacking onto the host (`cp` out of
  a microVM, a build's output) does the same. buildx follows such links, so here shards differs
  from Docker on purpose (5fc9b4f38, c2051c4bc).
- **PCI devices for programs that don't run as root** (Linux). A device plugged into a running
  microVM now belongs to the user the program runs as, and so does `/dev/iommu` while it is
  plugged. Before, a program run as the image's own user got "Permission denied" (D214;
  a45a7fca8, 72a2d1864).
- **Volumes plugged into a running microVM** could be removed or pruned under it:
  `shards remove volume` and `shards prune volume` judged a volume's use by the microVMs that
  mount it alone, and a plug is no mount. Now a volume any run holds, mounted or plugged, a
  daemon's run or a program's, is refused removal, `-f` or not (`cannot remove volume NAME:
  in use by a run that mounts or plugged it, or by another removal`; 409 from the Engine API),
  and a prune passes it by (D281; f00c87453, 75e34eb66).
- **Group budgets on macOS and Windows** (`--cgroup-parent`). A microVM joined to another's
  network runs in that microVM's VM process, and a group holding both counted that process
  twice: 1300 MiB where 700 were held. A member that grew between the group reading it and
  telling it its room had that growth counted twice too. Each VM process is now counted
  once, and a member's room is held as of what it held when it was read (D183; ea6c77a68,
  4a23a491e). A group's `cgroup.procs` and `memory.current` were each emptied and then
  written in place, so a reader could find one empty, or a sum beside the last pass's
  processes; each is now written beside it and renamed over it, the processes before their
  sum. Reproduced and fixed on macOS; on Windows it compiles but hasn't run (D183;
  adf292601). And a member's refused room could be lost as its give-back began, leaving its
  group's round waiting on that give-back, as two threads happened to run: a refusal is now
  its round's answer too, heard in order with every other, and a round its answers have
  finished is judged before the next pass. CI's macOS and Windows runners found it (D183;
  4c0828742).
- **A microVM that other runs joined could outlive its run and its daemon.** Where a run
  joined to its network (`--network container:NAME`) was ending as the microVM's own command
  ended, shards could ask the microVM to keep its writable layer in place, which such a
  microVM never does, and each then waited on the other for good: in CI two such microVMs
  were still running 20 minutes after their daemons had ended. A microVM any run has joined
  now sends its layer to its host (D119); what that costs one whose joined runs all ended
  long before it, which could have kept its layer in place, is unmeasured. And once its run
  is over and its daemon gone, a microVM now ends, whatever its joined runs still do:

## Known limits

- **arm64 Windows**: the full test suite has not passed yet, and it has run only on an
  emulated host, where microVMs of 64 vCPUs on its 4 CPUs run very slowly (D209, PM M427). It
  has not run on arm64 Windows hardware. GPUs haven't been tested there, and a host's PCI
  device given to a microVM there is built but untested, as on x86_64 Windows.
- **arm64 Linux**: of the decisions in the table above, only the engine's ring (D230) and
  PCI functions (D214) have run there, nested on a Mac. CI's arm64 Linux runners have no KVM.
- **x86_64 Windows**: most runs the table lists as owed include WHP's. Whether the
  agent-control connection's TLS ends at the host is decided from macOS and Linux runs;
  Windows' run is owed (D238). vsock connections' named pipes have run on x86_64 WHP; what
  each way of a connection's pipes holds before its writer waits, 256 KiB, is what a socket
  buffers on the other hosts, unmeasured over pipes, and what a connection's four handles
  cost is unmeasured too (D272).
- **Credentials** (D242): the injector's tests have run with real microVMs on
  Hypervisor.framework and x86_64 KVM, and on CI's x86_64 Linux and Windows runners; not on
  arm64 Linux or arm64 Windows. Codex's sign-in tests through the injector are written and
  haven't run. Claude Code's, Codex's and goose's sign-ins haven't been tried with real
  accounts, and what the injector costs a request or a connection isn't in the measurements
  record yet: its A/B harness is built.
- **Metrics** (D229) are printed as an OpenTelemetry request, not sent: shards has no
  exporter that delivers them yet. The run's, the workload's and each member's figures
  overlap, so they don't add up, and they are what their cgroups count, not all that a
  microVM costs its host. They have run on x86_64 KVM alone.
- **Saved microVMs**: the refusal of a microVM saved by 0.7.0 is unit-tested; its run with
  0.7.0's own binary is owed (D176).
- **Volumes** (D281): two members changing one file's length at once each count from the
  length they saw, so racing writes and truncations of one file can pass a volume's bound by
  what the other changed, until the volume's next first holder counts its files again. A
  removed file still open in a microVM that was killed keeps its charge until then too. On
  Windows the shared count is unverified, and the bound's cost, and its distance from what
  `du` says, are unmeasured, as is what holding a run's anonymous volumes costs.
- **Networks shared by runs** (D285) link the runs of one program: a network the daemon's runs
  are on, or another program's, is refused. A network with IPv6 is refused. What a flow, a
  link, a connect, a network's making and removal, and the other runs' telling as a run ends
  cost is unmeasured, so a link's table of flows has no bound learned from it yet.
- **A member's share of its run's memory** isn't re-derived as the run's budget changes: that
  change was held back when nesting tests failed with it on KVM. Its reproduction since points
  at the host's own file-system work rather than at the change, which A/B runs are still to
  show (D275, PM M605; 4124d8c70).
- **Moves.** Between two Macs, and over Wi-Fi, moves have not been measured with a baseline,
  and two-machine moves haven't been run again since links lost their fixed time-outs.
  Live moves on arm64 Linux and arm64 Windows have not been run with real microVMs, and how
  long a live move on Windows stops its microVM has not been measured. A volume's files are
  still sent with the final pass, so large volumes lengthen the time the microVM is stopped.
- **Contexts.** Docker's CLI through a context still serves none of its client's folders, as
  its API names a path alone. A client's binds have been measured over loopback only (D228),
  and contexts between machines running different builds of shards, 0.7.0 and 0.8.0 among
  them, haven't been tested.
- **PCI devices.** Windows' device assignment, on x86_64 and arm64, is untested on hardware
  that allows it. Linux's on x86_64 has been tested only with an emulated test device.
  Devices whose memory sits above 4 GiB can't be plugged yet. macOS offers no device
  assignment.
- **IPv6**: name lookups and published ports still reach agents over IPv4.
- **GPUs on musl** use Mesa's drivers only: NVIDIA ships no driver for musl.

the pools test's spacing (e80ae3775), the give-back test's falls (86432a12d), and arm64
Windows' test build (93a1741df).

- **Booting 255 vCPUs on x86_64 Linux** (D261). Since the change that brought `hvc0`, on
  CI's x86_64 Linux runners, where KVM runs nested under Hyper-V, a 255-vCPU guest's
  bring-up failed its VM entry (`hardware reason 0x7`) in 6 of the 8 test jobs that reached
  it. The snapshot tests' 255-vCPU storms failed so too, and other guests on the runner
  failed so only at that moment. Only Intel hosts can show it, and it hasn't been
  reproduced. Lane boot-ab: on an Intel runner the test now boots the guest a second time
  with its CPUs brought up one at a time, so that CI's next run shows whether their coming
  up all at once is the trigger (5fb2ec5ef); the remedy is then D261's to decide.
- **Booting 255 vCPUs on x86_64 Windows** (D261). On a nested WHP runner of 4 CPUs such a
  boot didn't reach its console in 3,170 s, where bare-metal WHP of 32 CPUs reached it in
  11 s, and the test waits on to its step's limit. Lane boot-ab had the test say each line
  of the guest's boot as it comes (5fb2ec5ef), and the test now judges such a boot by the
  kernel lines its guest has said (583c9e384, above): one that stalls is refused past the
  slowest gap its host has shown, with every line it said. Until a host has passed such a
  boot once, a guest that stalls before its lines can be read is still waited on to the
  step's limit there.
- **The snapshot tests' 255-vCPU storms on x86_64 musl** (an AMD runner, where 0x7 can't
  come): they ran on until the job's test step reached its 58-minute limit, and one storm's
  stream broke in its eighth round ("Broken pipe"), where the same storms of 2 and 8 vCPUs
  kept theirs whole. Both storms had booted 255 vCPUs at once on the runner's 4 CPUs. Each
  now boots 2 vCPUs and as many as the host has CPUs, and then the restores' storm the most
  a machine may have and the resumed one a vCPU more than its host's CPUs. The broken
  stream was that of the microVM the restores' storm restores, cut as it stopped once saved,
  as the test plans: it was read as the resumed storm's because both said only "255 vCPUs",
  and each storm's lines now name their storm and microVM (D14; 2726a8deb, 8cda6ff4c). CI
  hasn't run them since.
- **A managed guest's CPU rate on x86_64 KVM** (D265). The boot test of it counted 2.37 ms
  more guest time over 2 s than its quarter-CPU bound allows on glibc, and went past its
  bound on musl too. Whether KVM's guest time, counted in whole scheduler ticks,
  over-counted, or its governor let the vCPU run over, is not settled. Lane ci-fixes had the
  test read a precise count beside it and say which passed (07aa5aa5e); ROOT's runs under
  load tell.
- **`rm` of a run whose microVM isn't ready yet** (x86_64 Linux, glibc). The containers test
  of it hung to its step's limit, leaving no dump, and no cause was found in the code. Lane
  ci-fixes had a stuck wait in it refused, saying its daemon's log and processes
  (07aa5aa5e); ROOT's runs owed.
- **Waits judged under load** (Linux). The budget test
  `a_slow_start_is_refused_only_past_a_whole_slice_without_progress_or_hold` failed on arm64
  (glibc and musl) and x86_64 musl: its held stand-in's total, written each 5 ms by a thread
  the loaded host could leave unrun, was found unchanged by a look 30 ms after the one
  before. The stand-in now reads that total at each look as the kernel sums one (D188;
  47ef4e946). On CI's loaded x86_64 musl runner, other tests failed run after
  run: in run 43968984258, seven engine tests refused their judged waits, the daemon's test
  of its pools failed, and the containers test of a microVM giving back what it holds cold
  at its host's word found 871,706,624 bytes held idle and 901,070,848 after its read; in
  run 43950458285, the containers tests of export and rmi refused theirs. Four of those
  engine tests read an answer due later by design, an events stream asked to run until a
  time or a microVM's wait, whose head, sent at once, had taught the judge to expect its
  body as soon: a body is now judged apart from its head, and a stream asked until a time
  is waited on unjudged until then (bebc45a81). Two others were refused on answers' heads,
  a create's and a disk usage's, by a daemon that had run nothing for 5.7 and 6.3 ms. One
  had its running member's connect to a network refused by the daemon, judged by what the
  network's making had taken, as every verb of a network's switch shared one record of
  answers: each verb now has its own (D215; 22c7d141a), and whether that guest idled in its
  own kernel or its host didn't run it is still to be shown. The pools test's own
  keep-alive, 15 s, was under that host's refill window, which only the daemon measured, so
  the test spaced its runs blindly until its keep-alive refused them: the daemon now says
  its window as it times each refill, and the test spaces its runs and keeps by it (D26;
  e80ae3775). The give-back test took its idle before the give-back had settled, as a dip of
  its VM process's own memory could end that wait (which dip did there is unproven): a fall
  now counts only as a drop of a reported block, 2 MiB (D209; 86432a12d). Whether the loaded
  runner or a regression did the rest is ROOT's to settle.
- **The comparison with Firecracker** (x86_64 Linux). Its Envelope step, which holds shards
  no worse than Firecracker on any axis, has found shards' file-backed memory at boot (its
  PSS and RSS) worse than Firecracker's since 78753d965, on every CPU of CI's runners. Its
  samples were rounded to 0.1 MiB, and are now compared as measured (0080a20e5). Shards' VM
  process did grow: the code it maps went from 1,949,696 bytes on 2026-11-11 to 2,371,584 on
  2026-11-16, and what of it a boot keeps resident from 1,687,552 to 2,240,512, where
  Firecracker v1.17.0 maps 2,527,232 and keeps 2,002,944. What grew is its own code, for
  this release's features, not a dependency. What the smaller VM process (above) saves of it
  is unmeasured, and the Envelope has yet to run with it (D36; 13fc49770). Its Arrival step
  hung to its 30-minute limit in two runs whose logs weren't kept, so why is unknown: it now
  waits on each rate's requests as the tests wait, and a stall is said, naming the requests
  that never ended and what their home shows, and fails (b713e66b2).
- **An orchestrator's run hosting its lanes** (x86_64 Linux, musl).

- **x86_64 Windows**: both jobs of run 43968984258 ran to their test step's 122-minute

- **Removals that end together** (arm64 Linux, glibc). In run 43990298220 the daemon's test

- **A daemon's stop and its pending run** (x86_64 Linux, glibc). In run 43990298220 the
